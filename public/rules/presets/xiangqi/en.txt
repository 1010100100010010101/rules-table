1.1 Xiangqi (Chinese chess) is played by two players, Red and Black. Red moves first.
1.2 You win by checkmating the opponent's General, or by leaving the opponent with no legal move.
2.1 The board has 9 vertical lines and 10 horizontal lines. Pieces are placed on the intersections.
2.2 The middle band that no vertical line crosses is the River, which separates the two sides.
2.3 The 3×3 area with diagonal lines in the middle of each back edge is the Palace.
2.4 Each side has 16 pieces: 1 General, 2 Advisors, 2 Elephants, 2 Chariots, 2 Horses, 2 Cannons, and 5 Soldiers.
2.5 Set up in the standard positions: on the back line, from left to right, Chariot, Horse, Elephant, Advisor, General, Advisor, Elephant, Horse, Chariot; Cannons on the third line, on the second vertical line from each side; Soldiers on the fourth line, on every other vertical line.
3.1 Players take turns moving one of their own pieces.
3.2 Moving a piece onto a point occupied by an opposing piece captures it; remove it from the board.
3.3 You cannot move onto a point occupied by your own piece.
4.1 The General stays inside the Palace and moves one point horizontally or vertically.
4.2 The two Generals may not face each other on the same vertical line with no pieces between them ("flying general").
4.3 Advisors stay inside the Palace and move one point diagonally.
4.4 Elephants move exactly two points diagonally and cannot cross the River.
4.5 An Elephant cannot move if the point midway along its diagonal is occupied ("blocking the elephant's eye").
4.6 Chariots move any number of points horizontally or vertically but cannot jump over pieces.
4.7 Horses move one point horizontally or vertically, then one point diagonally outward.
4.8 A Horse cannot move in a direction if the point next to it in that direction is occupied ("hobbling the horse's leg").
4.9 When not capturing, a Cannon moves like a Chariot.
4.10 To capture, a Cannon must jump over exactly one piece (of either side) between it and its target.
4.11 Before crossing the River, a Soldier moves one point forward only.
4.12 After crossing the River, a Soldier may move one point forward or sideways, but never backward.
5.1 A move that threatens to capture the opponent's General on the next move is "check."
5.2 A player in check must get out of it immediately, for example by moving the General, capturing the attacking piece, or blocking.
5.3 You may not make a move that leaves your own General in check.
5.4 If you are in check and cannot escape, you are checkmated and lose.
5.5 If you are not in check but have no legal move, you also lose.
6.1 A player may not give check with the same moves over and over; the player making perpetual check must change moves or lose.
6.2 If neither player can checkmate the other, they may agree to a draw.
